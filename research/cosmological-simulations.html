Cosmological Simulations

Simulations of cosmic structure, and the numerical methods that run them.

In brief

Simulations connect theory with observation, but only if they are compared like with like: a simulated galaxy cluster has to be "observed" the way a real one is before the two can be compared. The numerical methods inside simulations matter too, because they decide what a simulation conserves and how far it can be trusted.

What I have done

Cosmological simulations are where I made my research debut, as a visiting undergraduate student at MIT in 2019. Using the Mock-X framework, we studied how galaxy clusters are classified as dynamically relaxed. We examined nine commonly used morphological metrics in 54,000 synthetic X-ray images of clusters from the IllustrisTNG, BAHAMAS and MACSIS simulations, and found that relaxed subsets defined by different metrics agree poorly, so selecting "relaxed" clusters introduces significant selection effects (Cao et al. 2021).

During my PhD, I also developed ContEvol, a family of implicit numerical methods that only need to solve linear equations and are almost symplectic. They give continuous solutions, and they handle problems from orbits to the Schrödinger equation (Cao 2025; code).

What comes next

ContCosmos: I have always wanted to return to cosmological simulations, including N-body simulations. Now that I know the statistics of large-scale structure much better, I can see more clearly what we can learn from them, and I plan to explore whether ContEvol can be a way to formulate them.
