Research · Track 3

3 · Cosmological Inference cosmology

What surveys like Roman's can tell us about the Universe, and how to extract it.

In brief

Roman's High Latitude Imaging Survey will test cosmological models with three related measurements: cosmic shear (the lensing of distant galaxies by the matter in front of them), galaxy–galaxy lensing, and galaxy clustering. Combined, they are known as a "3×2pt" analysis. Before the data arrive, forecasts tell us how tightly the survey can constrain cosmological parameters and which choices in the analysis matter most.

What I have done

As part of the Roman HLIS Cosmology Project Infrastructure Team's first data challenge, I led Fisher forecasts of the constraints on the parameters σ8 and Ωm from combinations of these probes (Cao et al. 2026). The forecasts agree well with a full Markov chain Monte Carlo analysis. Under the challenge's assumptions, galaxy–galaxy lensing plus clustering constrain these parameters more strongly than cosmic shear alone; the smallest angular scales and the highest redshifts carry the most information; and factor-of-two changes in the priors on photometric-redshift and shear biases change the figures of merit by 20% or less. I also contributed this experience with Fisher forecasts to a study of model approximations for Roman and Rubin.
