Research · Track 1

1 · Image Reconstruction images

Reconstructing well-sampled images with a controlled point spread function from a telescope's undersampled exposures, for the Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope and beyond.

In brief

The Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope will image the sky at unprecedented quality, yet its raw exposures are undersampled: the pixels are too coarse to capture the finest detail the optics deliver. To recover it, the telescope shifts slightly between exposures (dithering), and the exposures are later combined into well-sampled images.

How they are combined matters. Weak gravitational lensing, the main cosmological probe of Roman's High Latitude Imaging Survey, measures tiny distortions in the shapes of distant galaxies, so the reconstructed images need a point spread function (PSF) that is known and uniform. Time-domain surveys need reliable reference images too. Widely used methods such as Drizzle give limited control over the output PSF; IMCOM and its successor Effortless are designed to control it.

I treat this software as a scientific instrument: its design, testing and calibration decide what the data can tell us, as much as the telescope does.

What I have done

I led the refactoring of IMCOM into PyIMCOM: object-oriented code with improved data structures and new linear algebra strategies, which reduced the computing cost by about an order of magnitude (Cao et al. 2025). I then studied its hyperparameters systematically, re-coadding the same simulated fields with 26 configurations in five bands and comparing 12 criteria (Cao et al. 2026). PyIMCOM produced the IMCOM coadds of OpenUniverse2024, a shared simulated sky for Roman and Rubin, and it is the platform for several team papers (see Track 2).

A study in one dimension (Cao and Roman HLIS Cosmology PIT 2026) led to a new algorithm, first called Fast IMCOM and now Effortless (EFFicient Optimal image ReconsTruction using LESS memory). By avoiding IMCOM's most laborious calculations, it is tens of times faster and has a smaller memory footprint, and it can reconstruct a single exposure as well as many (Cao 2026a, Cao 2026b). The code is public on GitHub.

Like IMCOM, Effortless assumes that the astrometry, flux calibration and PSF models it is given are correct. In reality all of them have errors, which is one reason for the work ahead.
