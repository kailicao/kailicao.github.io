6 · Asteroseismology stars

Stellar oscillations as precise measures of stellar masses and ages, from Kepler to Roman.

In brief

Red giants oscillate, and the frequencies of their oscillations reveal their interiors. Simple scaling relations turn two measured frequencies into a star's mass and radius, and so its age, for many thousands of stars at once. For the most luminous giants, however, these relations show systematic offsets that have to be corrected.

What I have done

With Marc Pinsonneault, I proposed an alternative way to correct the scaling relations for luminous giants, which makes the results much less sensitive to how mean density is inferred from the oscillation frequencies (Cao and Pinsonneault 2025). In the α-rich population, it reduces the mismatch in median mass between the lower and upper red giant branch from 6.65% to 1.72%, and in median age from −21.8% to −9.6%. I also contributed to the APOKASC-3 catalog, especially the uncertainties of its spectroscopic measurements, and to related studies of red giants, including forecasts of asteroseismic yields for Roman's Galactic Bulge Time Domain Survey. I am a member of the Roman Wide-Field Science Team for asteroseismology with that survey.
