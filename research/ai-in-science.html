0 · AI in Science methodology

Using physics to improve the data that AI learns from, and making sure that everything AI helps produce can be checked.

In brief

Machine learning is increasingly used to analyze astronomical images, but a model learns whatever is in its training data, including the telescope's imperfections: a blurred or uneven point spread function, correlated noise, artifacts from undersampling. A model trained on such data may learn the instrument rather than the sky.

My approach is data-centric. Rather than building ever more complex models to compensate for imperfect data, I use physics to improve the data first, so that AI discovers the universe, not the instrument. Image reconstruction (Track 1) is where this starts: Effortless is a transparent, linear method whose output can be checked, which makes it a natural first step before any learned model.

What I have done

This track began with my Schmidt AI in Science Fellowship at MIDAS. Its first deliverable is MapHub, the MIDAS arXiv Preprint Hub, which ranks each day's new arXiv preprints in astrophysics and AI by their relevance to participating researchers. A language model assigns the scores; everything else is open: the rubric, the code and the daily tables, with every score labeled as AI-generated. The model's reasoning may be opaque, but what MapHub delivers can be checked.

Next

AI as a way to help reach the goals of the other tracks. For example, a method I plan for handling depth variation across a survey footprint (Track 3) relies on a machine-learning model.
