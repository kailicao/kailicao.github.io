5 · Stellar Modeling stars

Testing stellar evolution models against thousands of observed red giant stars.

In brief

Stars are the clocks and scales of the Universe: their ages date the history of the Milky Way, and their masses and sizes calibrate much of astronomy. Both depend on models of stellar structure and evolution, and large, homogeneous data sets now allow those models to be tested stringently.

What I have done

With Marc Pinsonneault, I compared stellar models computed with MESA against APOKASC-3, a joint spectroscopic and asteroseismic catalog of red giants in the Kepler fields (Cao and Pinsonneault 2026; code, data). Two diagnostics show robust tensions. In the first dredge-up, which brings nuclear-processed material to the surface, the observed change in [C/N] is smaller than predicted, by about 0.16 dex. The red giant branch bump is observed about 0.15 dex higher in surface gravity than predicted; envelope undershooting, a proposed fix, would destroy more lithium than observed, so lithium becomes a sensitive test. I have also helped maintain and modernize the Yale Rotating Stellar Evolution Code (YREC), as a coauthor of its public data release.
