Research · Track 2

2 · Image Analysis images

The physical meaning of astronomical images: noise, detector effects and systematics, and images as discrete samples of continuous fields.

In brief

Before images can be reconstructed or measured, their imperfections must be understood. Roman's infrared detectors add correlated noise and other effects, and in undersampled images, noise and artifacts can mimic the weak lensing signal that cosmologists want to measure. This track is about the images themselves: what their pixels mean and how their systematics can be modeled and removed.

What I have done

I have no first-author papers in this track yet. I have contributed to team studies of noise and systematics in simulated Roman images: how noise biases weak lensing measurements, how to remove correlated noise stripes, and how to make photometry robust using both Roman and Rubin images. My contribution has mainly been PyIMCOM, the platform for several of these studies, and expertise in IMCOM. The track grew out of my image reconstruction work (Track 1), which led me to think about the physical meaning of astronomical images.
