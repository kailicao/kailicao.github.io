Image Analysis

The physical meaning of astronomical images: noise, detector effects and systematics, and images as discrete samples of continuous fields.

In brief

Before images can be reconstructed or measured, their imperfections have to be understood. Roman's infrared detectors add correlated noise, such as horizontal stripes, and in undersampled images, noise and artifacts can mimic the weak lensing signal that cosmologists want to measure. This work is about the images themselves: what their pixels mean, and how their systematics can be modeled and removed.

What I have done

I have no first-author papers on this topic yet. I have contributed to team studies of noise and systematics in simulated Roman images: how noise biases weak lensing measurements; how to remove correlated noise stripes, which the team's imDestripe suppresses the stripes' power by factors of 10 to 30 on large scales; and how to make photometry robust using both Roman and Rubin images, where ignoring correlated noise underestimates photometric uncertainties by up to about three times. My contribution has mainly been PyIMCOM, the platform for several of these studies, and my expertise in IMCOM.

It grew out of my work on image reconstruction. Developing Effortless made me think hard about the physical meaning of astronomical images, and about the relationship between discrete samples and continuous fields.
