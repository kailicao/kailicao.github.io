Resources · Software

Software

Most of my research ends up as code. I release it under open licenses, with documentation, so that others can check and reuse it.

Codes

  • Effortless (Python, MIT license). Linear image reconstruction with a controlled point spread function, tens of times faster than IMCOM and with a smaller memory footprint. I designed and wrote it. Papers: Cao 2026a, Cao 2026b; background in Cao and Roman HLIS Cosmology PIT 2026.
  • PyIMCOM (Python, MIT license). Image combination for the Roman Space Telescope, developed by the Roman HLIS Cosmology Project Infrastructure Team, with tests, continuous integration and documentation. I led the refactoring of IMCOM into this object-oriented package, which reduced the computing cost by about an order of magnitude, and I added a configuration interface and docstrings to make it easier to use (Cao et al. 2025). It produced the IMCOM coadds of OpenUniverse2024.
  • MapHub (Python). Ranks each day's new arXiv preprints by their relevance to participating researchers, with a language model assigning the scores against a published rubric. I designed it and built it with AI assistance (Claude Code), including the scorer and the web viewer.
  • YREC (Fortran). The Yale Rotating Stellar Evolution Code. I have helped maintain and modernize it, as one of its contributors and a coauthor of its public data release (Pinsonneault et al. 2026).
  • mesa_apokasc (Python, MIT license). The MESA models and analysis behind my comparison of stellar models with APOKASC-3 red giants (Cao and Pinsonneault 2026), with the data on Zenodo.
  • ContEvol (Mathematica notebooks, MIT license). The examples for the ContEvol numerical methods (Cao 2025).
