About · Software

Software

Most of my research ends up as code. I release it publicly, mostly under open licenses and with documentation, so that others can check and reuse it.

Codes

  • Effortless (Python, MIT license). An algorithm for linear image reconstruction that, like IMCOM, controls the point spread functions of output images, but is tens of times faster and has a smaller memory footprint. Its programming interface is general enough for uses beyond weak lensing and beyond Roman. I designed and wrote it. Papers: Cao 2026a, Cao 2026b; background in Cao and Roman HLIS Cosmology PIT 2026.
  • PyIMCOM (Python, MIT license). Image combination for the Roman Space Telescope, developed by the Roman HLIS Cosmology Project Infrastructure Team, with tests, continuous integration and documentation. I led the refactoring of IMCOM into this object-oriented package, which reduced the computing cost by about an order of magnitude, and I added a configuration interface and docstrings to make it easier to use (Cao et al. 2025). It produced the IMCOM coadds of OpenUniverse2024.
  • MapHub (Python). Scores each day's new arXiv preprints in astrophysics and AI for their relevance to each participant, from a short research portfolio that each participant writes, and shows the scores as a heatmap: selecting yourself shows the papers that matter most to you, and selecting a paper shows who is most likely to answer questions about it. A language model assigns the scores against a published rubric. MapHub is a carpentry of the Schmidt AI in Science Fellowship that I lead, with teammates Kyle Heyblom, Eva Albalghiti and Dimitrios Simatos; I designed and built its scorer and web viewer with AI assistance (Claude Code).
  • YREC (Fortran). The Yale Rotating Stellar Evolution Code. I have helped maintain and modernize it, as one of its contributors and a coauthor of its public data release (Pinsonneault et al. 2026).
  • mesa_apokasc (Python, MIT license). The MESA models and analysis behind my comparison of stellar models with APOKASC-3 red giants (Cao and Pinsonneault 2026), with the MESA inlists (input files) and visualization notebooks on Zenodo.
  • ContEvol (Mathematica notebooks, MIT license). The examples for the ContEvol numerical methods (Cao 2025).
