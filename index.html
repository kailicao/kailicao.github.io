I help build the analysis infrastructure that next-generation sky surveys need, and use it to learn physics.

My PhD at The Ohio State University (2026), Renovating Analysis Infrastructure for the Next Generation of Astronomical Surveys, was a start on both. It contributed to computational foundations, such as image reconstruction for weak-lensing cosmology with the Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope and new numerical methods, and applied them to questions from red giant stars to cosmological forecasts. This site describes both what I have done so far and what I hope to build on it.

Research

My work falls into seven tracks. Track 0, AI in science, is the focus of my fellowship; Tracks 1–6 cover images, cosmology and stars.

  • 0 · AI in Science: Using AI to help with the research in the other tracks, while making sure that everything it produces can be checked.
  • 1 · Image Reconstruction: Reconstructing well-sampled images with a controlled point spread function from a telescope's undersampled exposures, for the Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope and beyond.
  • 2 · Image Analysis: The physical meaning of astronomical images: noise, systematics, and images as discrete samples of continuous fields.
  • 3 · Cosmological Inference: What surveys like Roman's can tell us about the Universe, and how to extract it.
  • 4 · Cosmological Simulations: Simulations of cosmic structure, and the numerical methods that run them.
  • 5 · Stellar Modeling: Testing stellar evolution models against thousands of observed red giant stars.
  • 6 · Asteroseismology: Stellar oscillations as precise measures of stellar masses and ages, from Kepler to Roman.
  • ✱ · Miscellany: Other work.
