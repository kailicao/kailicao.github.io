About

Position

Eric and Wendy Schmidt AI in Science Postdoctoral Fellow (2026–2028), Michigan Institute for Data and AI in Society (MIDAS), University of Michigan, in the 2026 cohort (my MIDAS profile). Mentors: Camille Avestruz (AI mentor, Physics; her group, ALCCA) and Feige Wang (science mentor, Astronomy; HURON).

Team memberships

  • Maximizing Cosmological Science with the Roman High Latitude Imaging Survey, a Roman Space Telescope Project Infrastructure Team led by Olivier Doré (2023–present): members · NASA team page
  • Asteroseismology Using the Galactic Bulge Time Domain Survey, a Roman Space Telescope Wide-Field Science Team led by Marc Pinsonneault (2024–present)
  • ALCCA (Avestruz Lab for Computational Cosmology and Astrophysics), the University of Michigan Physics group led by Camille Avestruz: Schmidt AI Fellow (2026–present)
  • HURON (High-redshift Universe Research and Observation in New Generations), the University of Michigan Astronomy group led by Jinyi Yang and Feige Wang: Schmidt AI Fellow (2026–present)
  • Center for Cosmology and AstroParticle Physics (CCAPP), The Ohio State University: graduate research assistant during my PhD (2023–2026)

Honors and funding

  • Eric and Wendy Schmidt AI in Science Postdoctoral Fellowship, University of Michigan, 2026–2028.
  • Research support as a member of NASA's Roman HLIS Cosmology Project Infrastructure Team and the Roman asteroseismology Wide-Field Science Team (as a PhD student).

Teaching and mentoring

Teaching

  • College Teaching (ESEPSY 7404), a graduate course at The Ohio State University, spring 2025. I planned a microteaching lesson by backward design, "Basic Counting: Permutations and Combinations," and taught it to my peers (slides, PDF).
  • Tutoring in college mathematics and physics and in French phonology, and pro bono help with graduate school applications (2022–2024).
  • I created and taught an online Python tutorial (2022–2023): 46 students in four cohorts. I gave the lectures and designed the exercises; its design is on Learning Python.
  • Teaching associate (grader), graduate quantum mechanics (Physics 7501 and 7502), The Ohio State University, 2021–2023: grading with written feedback to the whole class, and office hours for Physics 7502 in spring 2023.

Service

  • 2026–present: lead of MapHub, the MIDAS arXiv Preprint Hub, a carpentry of the Schmidt AI in Science Fellowship, with Kyle Heyblom, Eva Albalghiti and Dimitrios Simatos.
  • 2025: organizer of a PyIMCOM hack session for the Roman HLIS Cosmology Project Infrastructure Team, to help new users get started with the code.

Skills and software

  • Programming: Python (my main language; object-oriented, high-performance code with NumPy and Numba), C/C++, Fortran, Shell and Perl, Mathematica. Git and GitHub for all my projects.
  • Scientific software: designing algorithms and refactoring research code into maintainable packages; image processing; numerical methods; stellar evolution codes (MESA, YREC).
  • Survey pipelines and data products: image-coaddition software used in production by a Roman team (teammates ran PyIMCOM, which I refactored, to make the IMCOM coadds of the OpenUniverse2024 simulations, used in several team papers); configuration interfaces, documentation, onboarding and user support for a shared code: I have answered many of PyIMCOM users' questions and ran a hack session; a daily automated service with public data tables (MapHub).
  • Data analysis and inference: large simulated survey data sets, systematic studies of algorithm settings, validation against simulated truth, Fisher forecasts, statistical modeling; machine learning in my research, with data science training from the Erdős Institute (see Training below); large language models, applied in MapHub during my AI in Science fellowship.
  • Working with others: large collaborations (two Roman Space Telescope teams), code shared across a team, teaching and mentoring (see Teaching and mentoring).
  • Training: 31 credits of computer science and technology at Nanjing University (2020–2021); the Erdős Institute Data Science Boot Camp (2025).
  • Languages: Chinese (native), English, French.

Working with me

I am especially glad to talk about:

  • AI in science, and how to make AI-assisted results checkable;
  • image reconstruction, point spread functions and noise in survey images, including uses of Effortless beyond weak lensing and beyond Roman;
  • cosmological forecasts and inference for Roman and other surveys;
  • red giant stars: asteroseismology, stellar models, and Roman's Galactic Bulge Time-Domain Survey;
  • any of the ideas on my research pages that you would like to develop together.

Invitations. I am happy to give seminars and journal-club talks, in person or online, and to join collaborations on specific projects.

Students. I do not have funding to hire students or postdocs of my own, but I am glad to talk with students interested in these topics, to share what I learned applying for fellowships (see Career Development), and to help with Python (start with Learning Python). How I mentor is on Teaching Philosophy. If you are a student, in the US or elsewhere, and would like to do a research project on one of these topics, please write to me first. Where it would help, I can introduce you to my faculty mentors at Michigan, Camille Avestruz and Feige Wang, or to my PhD advisors at Ohio State, Christopher Hirata, Marc Pinsonneault and David Weinberg, and I may be able to put in a good word for you.

Writing to me. A short email that says who you are and what you would like to discuss works best. I usually try to reply within a few working days.
